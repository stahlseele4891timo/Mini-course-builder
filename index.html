Branding and Marketing Activity

Your brief: brand an unfamiliar airport

In this activity your team builds a marketing and branding plan for an airport. Each team is given a country, and you plan for an airport located there:

  • Team 1: Albania
  • Team 2: Papua New Guinea
  • Team 3: Azerbaijan
  • Team 4: Eritrea
  • Team 5: Paraguay

These countries were chosen on purpose. You probably hold no existing brand associations for any of them, so there is nothing to lean on. You have to build all four stages of the brand pyramid from nothing: salience (people know it exists), meaning (they know what it stands for), response (how they react to it) and resonance (a lasting bond with it).

You can plan for a real airport in your country, so research it quickly, or invent a plausible new gateway.

You may use AI, as the module's AI-use statement allows, but keep a log of your prompts. AI can invent airport facts, so check anything factual, such as runways and routes, before it goes into your plan.

What your team needs to deliver

Your plan is presented in Google Slides and has five parts:

  1. Positioning statement. Say who your target segment is and what the brand promises them. This is Step 1 of the process.
  2. Brand elements. Use as many as you sensibly can: a name, a logo concept, a slogan, a character or mascot, a URL, a jingle idea, and a signature packaging or visual identity.
  3. Resonance plan. Give one concrete initiative for each pyramid level, showing how you build salience, meaning, response and resonance.
  4. Marketing channels. Choose three channels aimed at consumers (B2C) and explain why, then add one business-to-business action aimed at airlines (Lesson 16).
  5. A secondary brand association. Pick one you will borrow from, such as the country, an event, a co-brand or licensing.

Each part matches one of the frameworks from Lesson 21, and your work will be marked against those frameworks rather than on creative taste.

Work in this order and keep to time. Spend about 10 minutes on research and positioning, 20 on brand elements, 15 on the resonance plan and channels, 10 on polish and 5 on rehearsing.

Pitches and judging

The last 30 minutes are for pitches. Each team has 4 minutes to sell the airport to the rest of the class.

Your peers score each pitch from 1 to 5 on three questions:

  • Coherence. Do all your elements tell one story?
  • Framework fidelity. Is every pyramid level genuinely addressed?
  • Feasibility. Could this airport actually carry out the plan?

Afterwards the class debriefs. Which element carried each brand? What did teams reach for first, and does that match how real brands are built?

Watch out for a common trap: jumping straight to logos and slogans. Those are brand elements, and the four-step process puts positioning first, so starting with them is backwards. The strongest pitch will show the correct order.
